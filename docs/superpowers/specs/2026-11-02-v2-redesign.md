# SMS Forwarder v2: interface, settings, rules and destinations

This spec extends [the v1 spec](2026-10-17-sms-forwarder-design.md). Anything not mentioned here
stays as in v1. Anything that is my decision rather than the user's is marked **(my decision)** and is
reviewed before the plan.

## 1. Why

v1 works, but it looks unfinished: settings behind a barely visible text gear, no navigation bar, no
"About", no icon of its own (it shows the Android robot), and the senders buried at the bottom of
Settings. It also only works for one server with a fixed format.

New goal: a general-purpose SMS forwarder that anyone can install and point at their own server. The
author's own use case is the first user and takes priority: nothing new may make it worse.

## 2. Goals, non-goals and anti-goals

Goals:
- Nothing below may lose an SMS. The inbox sweep, the persistent queue and the MIUI warnings keep
  their behavior unchanged.
- Everything is configurable from the app: destinations, rules, behavior, notices, appearance,
  language.
- Spanish and English.
- Own icon.

Non-goals (out of scope for v2): PIN or fingerprint lock, heartbeat, time windows, email, SMS to
another phone, forwarding calls or notifications from other apps, Play Store.

Anti-goals:
- A foreground service or a permanent notification.
- A migration that forces the current user to reconfigure anything.
- A badly templated body that sends SMS text to a site that is not the chosen one.

## 4. Icon

- An SMS bubble with a faint copy behind it (the original SMS) and the copy in front with a forward
  arrow drawn with a rounded stroke. Chosen sketch: "3, copy coming out".
- **Indigo** background color (green is confused with WhatsApp and blue with Google Messages).
- Adaptive icon for Android 8+, with a monochrome layer for the themed icons of Android 13+.
- Generated as a vector (`ic_launcher_foreground.xml`), with no raster images except the 512 px store
  icon for the releases.
- The sketches live in `.superpowers/` (ignored by git). The final vector is drawn in delivery 1 and
  shown to the user before committing.

## 5. Navigation

Bottom bar with 4 tabs and a ⋮ menu at the top right:

- **Home**, **Rules**, **Destinations**, **Settings**.
- ⋮ menu: **Export/Import** and **About**.

Deliveries 1 and 2: the bar has only **Home** and **Settings**, and the connection and the senders
live in Settings. From delivery 3 the bar is **Home**, **Rules**, **Destinations**, **Settings**; Rules
and Destinations are lists with a switch per row and a + button, and their editors open full screen
(Delete in the top bar).

Material 3, optional dynamic color, light and dark mode as in v1. SMS detail, onboarding and recovery
open as full screens without the bottom bar.

## 6. Home

As today: notices, status line, history. New:

- Search by text or sender.
- Filters by status (all, queued, error) and, from delivery 3, by destination.
- Detail: **Retry** (if there is an error), **Resend to a destination** (delivery 3) and **Delete**.
  Delete hides the SMS from the app and cancels its sending if it was still queued; it does not touch
  anything on the server.
  **(my decision)** The row is not removed: it is marked as deleted (`deletedAt`), because if it were
  removed, the sweep would find it again in the inbox and forward it again. Retention removes it for
  real once its period has passed.
- If "Hide text" is on (§7), the rows and the detail show the sender and the time, and the body is
  only visible after tapping "Show".

From delivery 3, each row shows the per-destination status: `Main ✓200 · Telegram queued`
(`Telegram ✗403` on error, `Telegram: check destination` if the configuration is invalid,
`Telegram: canceled` if the destination was deleted). A row of chips filters by destination.
**Resend to a destination** opens a dialog with the live destinations (paused ones marked) and accepts
one that already received the SMS. The detail shows an HTTP code only if it comes from a failing
delivery.

## 7. Settings

Groups, each with its own header:

- **Connection** and **Senders** existed in deliveries 1 and 2. In delivery 3 they no longer exist in
  Settings: they move to the Destinations and Rules tabs (onboarding still asks for them and creates
  the Main destination and exact rules).
- **Forwarding**
  - General switch "Forward SMS". Off: incoming SMS are still saved and stay in a paused queue,
    without trying to send. When turned on they are sent. **(my decision)** This way pausing never
    loses an SMS.
  - Automatic sweep every 15, 30 or 60 min (today 15).
  - "Stuck" notice after 1, 6 or 24 h (today 6).
- **History**
  - Retention of 30, 90 or 365 days, or forever. Default 90 days, as today
    (`QueuePolicy.RETENTION_MS`). Only rows already sent successfully are deleted; a queued or
    errored row is never deleted by age.
  - Hide the SMS text in lists.
- **Notifications**: turn each type on or off (recovered, stuck, permissions). The rejected-token
  notice (401/403) cannot be turned off, because it is the signal that sending is being lost.
- **Appearance**: theme (system, light, dark), dynamic color yes or no, language (system, Spanish,
  English).
- **Maintenance**: Recover SMS (what already exists), access to permissions and to the MIUI screens.

Changing a sweep setting reschedules the periodic work. None of them changes what is already in the
queue.

## 8. Rules

A rule decides which SMS are forwarded and to which destinations.

Fields:
- Name.
- Active yes or no.
- **Sender**: exact, contains or regex.
- **Text** (optional): contains, does not contain or regex. "Does not contain" forwards only messages
  without that text (case-insensitive). Use case: a bank sender also sends login notices; a rule with
  "does not contain" on the text of those notices lets payments through and discards the notices.
- **SIM**: any, 1 or 2.
- **Destinations**: one or more. A rule without live destinations is kept, does not count as active,
  and its row says so. Deleting a destination removes its link from the rules.

Sender comparison: same as v1 (no spaces and uppercase) in the exact and contains modes. In regex mode
the pattern is applied as is to the original sender, without normalizing; the user can use `(?i)`.
**(my decision)**

Regex: validated on save. An invalid pattern cannot be saved. The engine uses `find()` and limits the
time of each match to avoid catastrophic patterns; if it runs out, the rule does not match and this is
noted in the diagnostics. **(my decision)** This is the opposite of the reference app, which forwards
anyway if the body filter is invalid: here a broken filter cannot send an SMS to a place it does not
belong.

Evaluation: all active rules are evaluated. An SMS that matches several is sent **only once to each
destination** (union of destinations, no duplicates). An SMS that matches none is neither forwarded nor
saved, as today with a sender outside the list.

Removing or editing a rule does not cancel deliveries already queued. If a new rule does not match
SMS older than the sweep floor, those only get in through manual recovery (as in v1).

With no active rule: `NO_SENDERS` notice (renamed `NO_RULES`), like the current one.

## 9. Destinations

A destination is a place the SMS is sent to.

Fields:
- Name.
- Active or paused.
- Starting template (§10).
- URL, **https only** (same as v1).
- Method: POST, PUT or GET.
- Headers (key/value list). Those marked as secret are stored encrypted and never shown again.
- Body with variables.
- What counts as success (§11).

Variables: `{sender}`, `{body}`, `{received_at}`, `{received_at_ms}`, `{sim}`, `{device}`.

Substitution, **(my decision)**:
- If the body has type JSON, each variable is escaped as a JSON string before being inserted. An SMS
  with quotes, line breaks or `\` cannot break the body or inject fields.
- In the URL and in headers, variables are URL-encoded (line breaks are not allowed in headers).
- `{received_at}` keeps exactly the value and format that v1 uses (derived from `date_sent`, the same
  string on every retry). This is critical for the migrated destination: its deduplication depends on
  the string being identical between attempts.
- An unknown variable is rejected on save, not sent literally.

Save rules, delivery 3: the URL and the secret headers are never shown again (when editing, empty
field = keep what is saved); a GET destination cannot carry a body; if the body is JSON, it must parse
with the variables replaced by empty values or `0` (so an unquoted `{body}` cannot be saved). The
**main destination** is the live destination with the standard JSON template and the lowest id.

Test: a button that sends an invented test SMS (`TEST`, "test") to the destination and shows the
response code. It uses what is in the form, with the saved secrets where the field is empty; there is
no need to save first, and the test SMS is never saved nor left as an attempt. For the migrated
destination the current test is kept (body `{}`, 400 = correct), because a test SMS would pollute its
data. **(my decision)** The "standard JSON" template carries that test mode; the others send the test
SMS.

## 10. Destination templates

They only help fill in the form. Afterwards the destination is a normal destination.

- **Standard JSON**: POST, `Authorization: Bearer <token>`, body
  `{"sender":..., "body":..., "received_at":...}`. This is the v1 format.
- **Telegram bot**: asks for the bot token and chat id; the app builds the URL
  `https://api.telegram.org/bot<token>/sendMessage` and the body
  `{"chat_id":"<chat id>","text":"{sender}: {body}"}`. The token is part of the URL, so the
  destination URL is stored encrypted in full and is never shown or written to the log.
- **ntfy**: topic URL; POST with a text body `{sender}: {body}` and no headers by default.
- **Custom**: everything by hand; starts as POST, JSON, empty body.

## 11. Sending, queue and errors

Each **SMS + destination** pair is a delivery, with its own queue, attempts and status. A destination
that is down does not delay the others.

Success: any `2xx`, in all templates, including standard JSON. User decision (2026-11-02): this
section said only `200` for the standard template, but a `201` or `204` would be retried endlessly and
would duplicate the SMS on the server.

Errors, per destination:
- Network, timeout, `5xx`, `429`: retry with backoff, no limit, never dropped.
- `401`, `403`: no retry, immediate notification, only one per destination ("Token rejected at
  <destination>: N SMS waiting"). When new credentials are saved for that destination, its errored
  deliveries go back to the queue.
- `400`: no retry, one notification per delivery. Manual retry.
- Configuration error (template that cannot be rendered, URL or header that the HTTP client rejects):
  terminal, no retry. It records an attempt with no code and with error `config_<reason>`, the
  delivery ends in error and notifies like a 400. It goes back to the queue when the destination is
  saved.
- Other codes: transient.
- A `2xx` stores the real code (a `204` is shown as `✓204`).

Pausing a destination leaves its deliveries queued without trying them and does not touch those in
error. When it is resumed, its queued deliveries are scheduled immediately and those in 401/403 or
configuration error go back to the queue (without repeating what was already sent to another
destination). Fixing the token of a destination with 401/403 also requeues them; if the token changes
while a send is in progress, the late result neither marks an error nor notifies.

A deleted destination keeps its sent deliveries (with its name stored in the row). After confirmation,
its queued and errored deliveries become `CANCELED` (the notice says how many SMS are waiting);
`CANCELED` does not count in the aggregate status. An SMS whose deliveries are all canceled ends in
error with `destination_deleted`, does not raise a stuck notice, is purged with the normal retention
and can be resent to another destination. An SMS is stuck if a delivery to a live and active
destination is still queued past the deadline, or is in 400 or configuration error; the 401/403 ones
are covered by the token notice. `NOT_CONFIGURED` means that some destination used by an active rule
has no secrets.

The SMS identity (`localKey`, §5 of v1) does not change. Deduplication is still per SMS; delivery
uniqueness is `(sms, destination)`.

## 12. Migration (delivery 2)

When updating from 1.1.x to 1.2.0, with no user intervention and no network:

1. If there are saved credentials, a **Main** destination is created with the standard JSON template
   and that URL and token. **(my decision)** The name is generic; the user can rename it. The migrated
   destination treats any `2xx` as success.
2. Each sender in the list becomes an "exact" rule towards Main.
3. Each existing SMS gets a delivery towards Main with its current status. Historical attempts are
   kept.
4. Queued SMS stay queued and are sent as before.
5. The current settings (theme, sweep floor, indicators) are kept.

The migration is transactional and can be repeated with no effect. If it fails, the app starts in safe
mode: it does not send, it warns, and it leaves the database unchanged. It is tested with a real v1
database (exported schema) in `:app`.

Delivery 2 decisions (2026-11-02):

- **Main always.** The Main destination is always created when a destination is needed: in the SQL
  migration even if there are no credentials, and in a fresh install when the first sender is added or
  when credentials are saved. Without secrets it counts as NOT_CONFIGURED.
- **The unit of work is still the SMS.** `SendWorker` keeps `KEY_SMS_ID` and the unique name
  `send-<smsId>`, and sends that SMS's pending deliveries in parallel. This way the jobs queued by
  1.1.x keep working and a destination that is down does not block the others.
- **Aggregate status.** `sms.status`, `attempts`, `lastHttpCode`, `lastError`, `sentAt` and
  `stuckNotified` are kept as an aggregate of its deliveries, recomputed in the same transaction as
  each attempt.
- **Encrypted secrets.** The URL and the secret headers of each destination are stored encrypted.
- **`{sim}`** is the slot (1 or 2), `SubscriptionManager.getSlotIndex(subId) + 1`, empty if unknown.
  The SIM condition of a rule uses the same value.

The Room schema is versioned with an explicit migration and a committed schema file;
`fallbackToDestructiveMigration` is not allowed.

## 13. Export, import and diagnostics (delivery 4)

Export: a JSON file with destinations and rules. On export the user is asked whether to include
secrets; by default no. Without secrets, the destination is imported as "without credentials" and
warns.

Import: adds, does not replace. Shows a summary before applying. Destinations with the same name are
renamed with a suffix. Validates everything (https, variables, regex) before touching the database. An
invalid file changes nothing.

Export diagnostics: text with version, Android, permission and MIUI status, queue counters, errors by
code and the latest engine events. It never includes SMS text, full senders, URLs or secrets.

Implementation decisions (delivery 4):

- Format: JSON with `"format": "sms-forwarder/config"`, `"version": 1`, `"app"`, `"secrets"`
  (informational), `"destinations"` and `"rules"`. Each destination carries a `"key"` and the rules
  point to it, because ids are not valid across phones. A `version` greater than the known one
  invalidates the file; unknown fields are ignored and an unknown enum value invalidates it. Limits:
  1 MiB, 100 destinations, 500 rules and 50 headers per destination.
- Secrets: no secrets means no URL and no values for secret headers (their names are exported). Each
  destination decides for itself depending on whether it has a URL; the `secrets` field does not
  decide. A destination imported without a URL ends up "without credentials": `NOT_CONFIGURED` warns
  if an active rule uses it, and when its credentials are completed the queued SMS are scheduled
  immediately. Its secret headers are discarded and the summary names them. A destination without a
  URL can end up as the main one; the health notice covers it. The import is also atomic with the
  Keystore: if saving a secret fails, nothing is imported. Import keeps `enabled`.
- Renaming: suffix ` (2)`, ` (3)`…, ignoring case and surrounding spaces, against what already exists
  and against what is planned in the same file; also for rules.
- ⋮ menu: Export, Import and About. The first two are disabled while the legacy migration is in safe
  mode. The import shows a progress indicator and the summary uses plurals and the line of
  destinations without URL or token.
- Engine event log: file `engine-events.log` in `noBackupFilesDir` with the last 500 lines, without
  Room and without a schema migration (Room stays at version 3). It never records personal data; the
  diagnostics show the last 50.
- Diagnostics: destinations by id and template type, never by name; errors grouped by `http_<code>`,
  `network`, `config_<reason>`, `destination_deleted` or `other`. Each source fails separately and is
  marked as unavailable without breaking the report.

## 14. About

Icon (cropped to a circle), version, link to the code on GitHub, license and libraries used, with a
back arrow like the editors. Export diagnostics: a button that saves a text file
(`sms-forwarder-diagnostic-<YYYY-MM-DD>.txt`) with the Storage Access Framework, without new

## 15. Texts and languages

All texts move to `strings.xml` with `values/` (English, the default) and `values-es/`.
**(my decision)** English is the default language because the app is public; the author's phone stays
in Spanish. Selector in Settings with System, Spanish and English. **(my decision)** It does not use
the per-app language API of Android 13+ (`LocaleManager`/`AppCompatDelegate`): with `minSdk` 31 it
would force `AppCompatActivity` and its theme on Android 12, a visual change. Instead, a context
wrapper applied in `attachBaseContext`, with the chosen language also saved in SharedPreferences so it
can be read without waiting for DataStore, and the notification notices localized with the same
wrapper. When a language is chosen, the selector saves the setting (which updates the mirror and the
name of the notice channel) and calls `recreate()` on the activity; the navigation stack survives
because it is saved with `rememberSaveable`.

No SMS text or real sender name appears in strings, tests or docs.

## 16. Tests

- `:core` (JVM): rule evaluation, templates and substitution with escaping, regex and variable
  validation, success and error policy per destination, export/import. Hostile cases: SMS with quotes,
  line breaks, `\`, `{body}` inside the text, catastrophic regex, URL with `http`.
- `:app` (CI): migration 1.1 to 1.2 against a v1 database, deliveries per destination with Room, queue
  with several destinations.
- Manual checklist on the phone: the current one plus the icon in the drawer (light, dark, themed),
  pause and resume, a destination that is down without blocking another, language change, importing an
  invalid file.

## 17. Distribution

Signed GitHub releases, as now. F-Droid stays as a later idea and does not constrain v2. No Play Store
(Google's restrictions on apps that read SMS).
